Grammar Detective, UNIT / MASS
Narration: George (British English, male)

[0:00.35] Furniture. Luggage. Gold. Rice. Knowledge. Work. We’re told to memorise lists like these: no plural, singular verb.

[0:09.62] But what if you didn’t have to memorise them? What if you could prove it, with logic?

[0:15.42] Case one: furniture. Why is ‘The furniture are expensive’ wrong?

[0:20.78] Proof one. Point to a furniture. This? That’s a chair. This? A table. A sofa. A bed. Every object already has its own name.

[0:30.42] Nothing here is ‘a furniture’. So furniture is not the name of one object.

[0:37.32] Proof two. Furniture is the name for the whole group. Chair, table, sofa, bed: one box, one label.

[0:45.05] Try counting the label: one furniture, two furnitures? It fails. We count what’s inside: one chair, two chairs.

[0:54.35] Proof three. A group name works as one whole, so the verb is singular: ‘The furniture is expensive.’

[1:02.32] Need a number? The two won’t stick to furniture. Give it something countable: ‘Two pieces of furniture are expensive.’

[1:11.08] Now you try: luggage. Point to a luggage? No, that’s a suitcase, that’s a bag. Group name, so: ‘The luggage is heavy.’

[1:19.88] So don’t memorise. Ask: can I point to one of it? If not, it’s a group name. Treat it as one.
