Grammar Detective, STYLE SAMPLE
Narration: George (British English, male)

[0:00.35] Why is ‘The furniture are expensive’ wrong?

[0:05.12] Look at what furniture contains: a chair, a table, a sofa, a bed. Many different things.

[0:12.22] But English doesn’t name each one. It groups them together and names them as one unit: furniture.

[0:19.45] Why? Because furniture is not the name of an object. It’s the name of the whole collection. The word comes from ‘furnish’: everything you furnish a room with.

[0:29.82] And a unit means one. One is always singular. We can’t add s to one. So: ‘The furniture is expensive.’

[0:38.38] The same logic works for the whole family. Luggage: bags and suitcases. Equipment: tools and machines. Clothing: shirts and trousers. Crockery: plates and cups. Stationery: pens and paper.

[0:52.92] Notice the endings: luggage, equipment, clothing, crockery. These endings often build a word for a whole collection.

[1:01.52] So each one is one unit, with a singular verb: the luggage is heavy, the equipment is new, my clothing is wet.

[1:09.75] Need to count them? Count a piece: two pieces of furniture, three pieces of luggage, an item of clothing.

[1:17.68] So don’t memorise the list. See the unit: many things, one name, one verb.
