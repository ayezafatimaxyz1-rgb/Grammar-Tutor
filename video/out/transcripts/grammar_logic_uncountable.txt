Grammar Detective, Grammar Logic Series: Uncountable Nouns
Narration: George (British English, male)

[0:00.35] Uncountable nouns. Why can’t we add s to them? And why can’t they take a plural verb?

[0:07.32] Countable nouns are easy: a chair, two chairs, three ideas. We count them one by one, so they can take s.

[0:15.92] But what about water, rice, honesty, or music? Nobody says two waters, or three honesties.

[0:23.52] Most books just give you a list to memorise. We won’t. We’ll use logic. Uncountable nouns fall into five categories, and each category has its own reason.

[0:35.35] One: unit, or mass. Two: material. Three: measured. Four: abstract. And five: activity, or process.

[0:43.82] Let’s prove them one at a time.

[0:47.62] Error one. ‘Furniture are expensive.’ That’s wrong. The correct sentence is: ‘Furniture is expensive.’ But why?

[0:56.28] Category one: unit, or mass. This kind of noun is a unit, and a unit contains many things.

[1:04.18] Look. A chair. A table. A bed. A sofa. A desk. Many individual things.

[1:10.88] English groups them together. It draws one circle around them, and treats the whole group as one unit, called furniture.

[1:19.68] Why? Because furniture is not the name of one object. It’s the name of the whole collection. The word comes from ‘furnish’: everything you furnish a room with.

[1:30.45] And the word unit means one. One thing. And we can’t add s to one.

[1:35.85] One is always singular. We can’t add s to one, so the word stays singular, and so does the verb.

[1:43.22] So the correct sentence is: ‘The furniture is expensive.’

[1:48.52] The old school rule says: furniture, luggage, baggage and crockery are always singular. Memorise it. Skip that. Here’s the logic: many individual things are named as one unit. Unit means one, and one is always singular.

[2:04.88] The same logic works everywhere. Luggage: bags and suitcases together, one whole. Clothing: shirts and trousers, one category. Crockery: plates and cups, one collection.

[2:17.45] And if you need a number, count a piece: a piece of furniture, a piece of luggage, an item of clothing.

[2:25.12] Now think about it. Why can you say ‘two chairs’, but not ‘two rice’?

[2:31.02] Error two. ‘This ring is made of golds.’ Wrong. ‘This ring is made of gold.’

[2:37.22] Category two: material. A material is what something is made from. It’s a substance.

[2:44.28] Take gold. It’s a substance, and it’s used to make many different things: a ring, a coin, a crown.

[2:51.92] We can count the things: one ring, two coins. But the substance itself is measured, not counted. We weigh gold in grams.

[3:01.68] So a material noun, used as a material, isn’t made plural. ‘This ring is made of gold.’

[3:09.48] The old school rule says: brick, sand, milk, glass and water are always singular. Memorise it. Skip that. The logic: it’s the material used to make other things, so you measure it rather than count it.

[3:24.18] Silver: used for jewellery, measured, not counted. Wood: used for furniture, measured in cubic metres. Water: used for drinking, measured in litres.

[3:35.22] One careful note. When you mean separate objects, you can count them: a wall made of bricks, or two glasses of water. But as a material, it’s brick and glass, with no s.

[3:47.55] Now think about it. Do we say ‘one milk’, or ‘one litre of milk’?

[3:53.35] Error three. ‘I ate too many rice for lunch.’ Wrong. ‘I ate too much rice for lunch.’

[3:59.78] Category three: measured. Food and substances like rice are measured by amount, not counted as separate items.

[4:08.68] Rice comes as many tiny grains. We don’t count them one by one. We measure the amount instead: a cup of rice, a kilo of rice, a plate of rice.

[4:20.18] Measured, not counted, so it always takes a singular verb. ‘This rice is delicious.’

[4:27.55] Material and food nouns can look alike, so here’s the test: do we buy it in a measured amount, like a kilo or a litre, and not in pieces? If yes, it’s uncountable. The old school rule says food, meat, sugar, salt, tea and bread are always singular. The logic: they’re measured, not counted.

[4:47.42] Bread: measured in loaves or slices. Milk: measured in litres. Salt: measured by weight.

[4:54.98] Now think about it. Can you say ‘two rice’, or do you need to say ‘two kilos of rice’?

[5:02.05] Error four. ‘He has many knowledges about art.’ Wrong. ‘He has a lot of knowledge about art.’

[5:08.95] Category four: abstract. An abstract noun has no physical form. It can’t be held or touched.

[5:16.62] It’s an idea, a quality, or a state. Knowledge, honesty, advice, happiness. You can’t put them on a table and count them.

[5:25.68] You experience it, show it, or possess it, but you never count it. So it’s singular: ‘Her knowledge is impressive.’

[5:34.28] The old school rule says advice, information, evidence and knowledge are always singular. Skip that. The logic: we can’t physically hold them, or count them directly. Need a number? A piece of advice, a piece of information, a piece of evidence.

[5:51.35] Now think about it. Can you hold honesty in your hand, or can you only show it, have it, or experience it?

[5:59.82] Error five. ‘She does many works every day.’ Wrong. ‘She does a lot of work every day.’

[6:06.68] Category five: activity, or process. We talk about an ongoing process as a whole, not as separate actions.

[6:15.48] Work has many small steps: emails, calls, meetings, reports. But we talk about it as one ongoing whole.

[6:24.15] One process, not separate actions, so a singular verb. ‘Her research is impressive.’

[6:31.38] The old school rule says work, research, progress, travel and exercise are always singular. Skip that. The logic: you’re talking about the whole activity, not separate actions. Research: the whole process of studying. Travel: going from place to place. Shopping: the whole activity of buying things.

[6:52.08] Now think about it. Do we say ‘I do a work’, or ‘I do work’?

[6:57.68] Let’s put all the examples on the board.

[7:01.35] Unit or mass: furniture, luggage, baggage, equipment, machinery, garbage, rubbish, clothing, stationery, crockery, cutlery, scenery and traffic. Many things, one unit.

[7:13.98] Material: gold, silver, iron, copper, steel, wood, stone, sand, clay, cotton, wool, silk, leather, plastic, glass and paper. It’s what things are made of.

[7:25.55] Measured: food, rice, bread, butter, cheese, meat, sugar, flour, salt, milk, water, tea, coffee and honey. Measured by amount.

[7:34.62] Abstract: advice, information, knowledge, education, honesty, justice, patience, courage, wisdom, happiness, freedom, peace and evidence. No physical form.

[7:46.58] Activity or process: work, research, progress, travel, exercise, training, sleep, shopping and business. One whole activity.

[7:56.78] So don’t memorise the list. Find the category, and the logic tells you: no s, and a singular verb. That’s the Grammar Logic Series.
