Grammar Detective, Real life: one unit
Narration: George (British English, male)

[0:00.35] Quick question. Why do we never say furnitures, equipments, or luggages?

[0:06.68] Watch. Here’s an empty room.

[0:10.02] In comes a chair.

[0:13.88] Then a sofa.

[0:17.32] Then a bed.

[0:20.45] Now the room has furniture. Not three furnitures. Just furniture.

[0:26.05] English doesn’t name each piece. It groups them into one unit. And a unit means one. One is always singular, so no s: the furniture is new.

[0:37.05] Now a mechanic’s workshop, and an empty toolbox.

[0:41.52] A spanner goes in.

[0:45.25] Then a hammer.

[0:48.68] Then a drill.

[0:51.98] Close the lid. Many tools, one box, one unit: equipment. So: the equipment is ready.

[0:59.38] Now someone is getting ready for the airport.

[1:03.48] First, a suitcase.

[1:07.32] Then a backpack.

[1:10.95] Then a carry-on bag.

[1:14.58] Bags and suitcases together make one unit: luggage. So: my luggage is heavy.

[1:21.45] Need a number? Count a piece: two pieces of furniture, a piece of equipment, three pieces of luggage.

[1:28.88] So don’t memorise the list. See the unit: many things, one name, one verb.
