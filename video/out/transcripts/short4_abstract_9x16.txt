Grammar Detective Short: ABSTRACT
Narration: Emma (British English, female)

[0:00.35] Why ‘a lot of knowledge’, but ‘three ideas’?

[0:03.55] He knows many facts about art. But knowledge names his whole body of understanding, not each fact separately. So not ‘many knowledges’.

[0:12.52] Say: ‘He has a lot of knowledge about art.’ Advice works similarly: ‘some advice’, or count it as ‘two pieces of advice’.

[0:21.08] But an idea is abstract too, and ‘three ideas’ is correct. Abstract doesn’t decide it. Ask: does this word name one undivided body, or separate items?
