- Start each lesson from MY sentence, showing every choice I gave side by side, each marked: ✓ right, ≈ correct English but a different meaning, or ✗ wrong, with a one-line reason.
- Explain the difference between the words side by side (a table or columns), not one after another.
- Give each little word a simple PICTURE that shows its meaning (for example: at = a pin on one point, on = touching a surface, to = an arrow, into = an arrow going through a door).
- Teach on a hand-drawn whiteboard, step by step: the drawing appears line by line while the matching explanation sentence lights up beside it.
- Build every rule from the picture. Never just state a rule.
- Give everyday examples (Pakistan-friendly: rupees, lakh, Lahore, Mall Road, Eid, trains, shops).

- End each lesson with 3 practice questions. EVERY answer choice, right or wrong, must explain why.
- Wrong answers go to a mistake notebook, grouped by the misunderstanding behind them, and come back for spaced review.
- Finish each lesson with a short summary rule I can remember.

THE 8 LESSONS

1. "I can't buy this car FOR / AT this price."
- Both are correct.
- FOR = a swap: money in exchange for a thing. Best with a real amount: "I bought this car for 20 lakh." Pattern: buy/sell/get + thing + for + money.
- AT = a point on the price line. Best with "this price", a rate or a discount: "I can't buy it at this price", "at Rs 300 a kilo", "at half price", "at a 20% discount".
- Mistake: "in this price" is never correct.
- PAY trap: "I paid 20 lakh for the car" (pay + money + for + thing), not "I paid the car for 20 lakh".
- "At that price, it's a deal" (price level) vs "For that price, you could buy a new car" (what the money could be swapped for).
- Drawing ideas: a car and a money note with swap arrows (for); a cheap-to-expensive price line with a dot (at).

2. "Send this book TO / AT / ON my home address."
- TO = movement towards a place: "Send this book to my home address." (send, post, deliver, bring + to)
- AT = an exact spot, a full address with a house number: "I live at 25 Mall Road."
- ON = a street name with no number: "I live on Mall Road."
- IN = a city, area or country: "I live in Lahore."
- Mistakes: "send it on my address" and "send it at my address".
- Useful: "Send it to me at my home address." (to = the person, at = the exact spot)
- Drawing ideas: a book with an arrow to a house (to); a map pin on a house (at); a road (on); a big circle for the city (in).

3. AT / ON / TO / UPON, side by side
- AT = a point. Place: at the bus stop, at the door. Time: at 5 o'clock, at night, at noon.
- ON = touching a surface or line. Place: on the table, on the wall, on Mall Road. Time: on Monday, on 14 August.
- TO = an arrow, movement towards. Place: go to school, send it to Ali. Time: from 9 to 5 (= until).
- UPON = a formal or old-style "on". "Once upon a time", "upon arrival" (= as soon as you arrive), "depends upon" (= depends on). In everyday English, just say "on". Never "upon Monday".
- "Throw the ball AT Ali" = to hit him. "Throw the ball TO Ali" = so he can catch it. Also: shout at someone, talk to someone.

4. "I saw him felling the tree WITH / BY / THROUGH a hatchet."
- Answer: WITH a hatchet.
- WITH = a tool you hold: with a knife, with a pen, with a key.
- BY = the way something is done, with no "a" or "the" (by hand, by bus, by email), or who did it: "The tree was felled by Ali with a hatchet."
- THROUGH = in one side and out the other (through the window, through the tunnel), or a channel (through a friend, through hard work).
- Note: "felling" (cutting a tree down) is correct, not "falling" (dropping).

5. "I must start AT / ON / BY / AFTER dawn to reach the station in time."
- Best answer: AT dawn, because dawn is a point of time (also at noon, at night, at sunrise, at midnight).
- ON is for days and dates, so "on dawn" is wrong.
- BY dawn is correct English but means no later than dawn (a deadline).
- AFTER dawn is correct English but means later than dawn.
- IN + a part of the day: in the morning, in the evening. But "at night", and "on Monday morning" (a named day takes on).
- Drawing idea: a night-to-day time line with a rising sun as the dawn point, a "by" bracket before it and an "after" bracket after it.

6. IN TIME / ON TIME / AT TIME
- IN TIME = early enough, before it is too late: "We reached the station in time to catch the train", "in time for dinner", "just in time".
- ON TIME = exactly at the planned time, punctual: "The train left on time", "start on time", "pay the bill on time".
- AT TIME is never used alone. Correct phrases: "at the time" (= then, in the past), "at times" (= sometimes), "one at a time" (= one by one).
- Mistake: "Please come at time" should be "Please come on time".
- Drawing idea: a time line with a red "7:00 train leaves" line. A dot before it = in time (with spare time), a dot on it = on time, a dot after it = late.

7. "I have known him FOR / SINCE / FROM / OVER a long time."
- Answer: FOR a long time.
- FOR + a length of time: for 10 years, for two hours, for a long time.
- SINCE + a starting point up to now: since 2016, since Monday, since childhood.
- FROM + a start, usually with an end: from 2016 to 2021, open from 9 am to 6 pm. Not used for "up to now".
- OVER = across a whole period, often while something changes: "Over the years, we became good friends."
- Common mistakes: "since 10 years" and "from a long time". Both should be "for".
- Drawing idea: a time line with a pin at 2016 and an arrow to now (since), a bracket measuring the length (for), a closed bracket from 2016 to 2021 (from…to), and a slowly rising line (over).

8. "Will you walk INTO / IN / TO / BY my parlour?"
- This is the first line of the poem "The Spider and the Fly" by Mary Howitt. Answer: INTO.
- INTO = from outside to inside (movement): "He walked into the room", "jump into the pool".
- IN = already inside, no movement: "He is in the room", "swim in the pool".
- TO = towards a place, maybe without going inside: "walk to the shop".
- BY = past it without going in: "walk by the shop". It can also mean near: "sit by the window".
- Drawing idea: four rooms with a door. An arrow going through the door (into), a loop inside the room (in), an arrow stopping at the door (to), and an arrow passing the room (by).
